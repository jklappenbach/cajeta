# ML: estimators, layers and boosted trees

Two libraries, both written in Cajeta over [Nucleo](../nucleo/README.md) and
[Math](../math/README.md), published as `dev.cajeta.ml` and
`dev.cajeta.xgboost` and documented in their own repositories.

## `dev.cajeta.ml`

Two halves with one estimator protocol between them.

- **Classical**, the scikit-learn and statsmodels role over
  `Tensor<float64>`: linear models with real inference (`LinearRegression`
  by QR least squares with a `summary` of standard errors, t and p values,
  R² and F; `Ridge`, `Lasso`, `ElasticNet`, `LogisticRegression`), the
  discriminants, kernel regression, k-nearest neighbours, k-means and the
  other clusterers, embeddings and decomposition, preprocessing, model
  selection (`Split.crossValScore`, the searches), metrics and curves.
- **Neural**, the torch role over `Tensor<float32>`: define-by-run autodiff
  (`ml.grad`), modules and layers through transformers (`ml.nn`), optimizers
  and two trainers (`ml.train`, `ml.optim`), checkpoints and LoRA (`ml.io`),
  torch-checkpoint interop and transformer completion.

The **estimator protocol**: `Estimator` (`fit(x, y)` stores state on the
instance; hyperparameters are constructor arguments, never mutated),
`Predictor` (`predict`, `score`), `Transformer` (`transform`,
`fitTransform`). Model-selection code sees only `Predictor` and never names
a concrete type; sibling model libraries conform to it through an adapter.
Dataframe columns cross into estimators through nucleo's zero-copy seam.

Device status: as of 0.10.6 the neural half runs on the host; the device
variants in the benchmarks skip with that reason until the library's ops
gain kernels.

The documentation lives with the code: the
[index](https://github.com/jklappenbach/cajeta-ml/blob/main/docs/README.md),
the [guide](https://github.com/jklappenbach/cajeta-ml/blob/main/docs/Guide.md)
(the protocol, every model and utility, API shapes), the runnable
[tour](https://github.com/jklappenbach/cajeta-ml/blob/main/docs/Tour.md), and
the two honest difference lists against
[scikit-learn](https://github.com/jklappenbach/cajeta-ml/blob/main/docs/DifferencesFromSklearn.md)
and [PyTorch](https://github.com/jklappenbach/cajeta-ml/blob/main/docs/DifferencesFromTorch.md).

## `dev.cajeta.xgboost`

Gradient-boosted decision trees whose contract is **bit-exactness to
XGBoost's NVIDIA GPU `hist` algorithm**, on every backend Cajeta runs on:
given the same inputs and hyperparameters, the identical trees and
predictions, to the bit, on the CPU, on AMD and on NVIDIA, checked per
round, per tree and per node against fixtures generated by the reference.
Determinism is the default and any non-deterministic mode is opt-in and
recorded in the model. Its 33 device kernels and the one adaptation they
make are on [Gradient boosting on the device](../kernels-xgboost.md), with
the [catalog](../catalog-xgboost.md).

## Where they stand

The [benchmarks](../../../bench/ml/README.md) measure the classical
estimators against scikit-learn on the same data within recorded tolerances,
the neural models against PyTorch, and the boosted trees against upstream
XGBoost. On cajeta 0.46.3 several full-size rows are hours where the
references are seconds (k-nearest neighbours, logistic regression on the
digits set, an XGBoost CPU fit), and the pages will say so when the first
run is published.
