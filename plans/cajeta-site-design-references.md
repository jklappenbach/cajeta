## The gold standard — Aesop
`aesop.com` — cream/beige/black with brown accents; editorial, restrained,
typographic. The canonical proof that brown/cream/black reads as **premium,
intellectual, calm**.
- **Aesop "Aromatique Candles"** — Awwwards **Site of the Day** (7.53, Studio Regale)
- **Aesop "Taxonomy of Design"** — Awwwards **Honorable Mention** (Pollen)
- **Aesop.com redesign** (Work & Co) — **HOW International Design Award**
- *Borrow for Cajeta:* generous whitespace, eyebrow/serif typography, muted palette,
  one product (= one feature) as hero, calm "comfort + sophistication" tone — exactly
  Cajeta's pitch.

## Direction B — artisan-heritage (Affinity Creative / Buck Shack) ★ user-liked
Affinity Creative Group (premium wine/spirits branding; "artisan, organic, distinctive").
**Buck Shack** (Shannon Family of Wines) — from a restored 100-yr-old hunting shack →
a *lodge / barrel-aged* world: woodgrain, leather, kraft paper, letterpress/stamped
marks, badge-and-banner lockups, buck/antler iconography, vintage serif type, **gold/
foil accents** on deep brown/black.
- Links: affinitycreative.com/portfolio · behance.net/affinityCG · "Build Your Buck Shack" campaign.
- **Why it fits Cajeta:** cajeta = *homemade caramel* = artisan/comfort/craft. The caramel
  cube becomes a "product label" hero; the site can feel like a beautifully crafted tin.
- **Translate to the site (a "modern artisan" system):**
  - Logo: heritage **badge / wax-seal / crest** around the caramel cube; letterpress wordmark.
  - Type: warm display serif (Fraunces still works; or a more vintage/condensed serif) +
    small-caps eyebrows; clean body for legibility.
  - Texture: subtle kraft/paper grain, debossed cards, **a gold foil accent** for premium.
  - Motifs: stamp/seal, ribbon banners, "label" cards, optional woodcut-style diagram art.
  - Palette: deepen toward **espresso/near-black + cream + caramel + a metallic gold**.
  - Keep a **modern clean grid** under the texture so it still reads as a dev tool — crafted, not kitschy.

## Direction C — "hearth / Mexican artisan" ★ CHOSEN
The locked direction (personal + authentically on-brand): the world of **cajeta**
itself — a clay **olla**, caramel, a warm family **kitchen** (comal, cast iron),
**wood**, **organic** materials, **family, comfort, terracotta**. Takes the warmth/craft of
Direction B (Buck Shack) and grounds it in cajeta's real cultural roots. The full
texture/material system + palette extensions live in plan **§3.1 / §3.1.1**.
Sleek-minimal (Aesop/OXMAN) is retained only as a reference for *restraint and
legibility under the texture*, not for the mood.

## Trend context
**Pantone 2025 Color of the Year = "Mocha Mousse"** — a rich, earthy brown signaling
*stability + unpretentious sophistication*. Brown/cream is currently surging in luxury,
hospitality, and editorial design — good timing for Cajeta's identity.

## What the acclaimed ones share → apply to Cajeta
1. **Restraint:** 2–3 colors (cream canvas, espresso text, one caramel accent) + heavy whitespace.
2. **Editorial typography:** a characterful display face (often a warm serif — cf. our Fraunces choice) + clean body; big type, tight leading.
3. **Warm neutrals as the canvas**, not loud color — richness comes from imagery/gloss (our caramel cube).
4. **One tactile hero moment** — for us, the glossy/animated caramel cube.
5. **Material metaphor** (beige = paper, brown = earth) — ours writes itself (cajeta = caramel).
